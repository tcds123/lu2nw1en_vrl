Maintain Overall Clarity and Avoid Contradiction

**When to apply**: For any prompt, as a final review step. Specifically, when instructions are long, use double negatives, or contain phrases that might conflict (e.g., "make it efficient" followed by "use this naive algorithm").
**What to do**: Read the prompt critically. Simplify sentence structure. Remove unnecessary adjectives and adverbs. Ensure all technical instructions are logically compatible. Replace "should not avoid" with "must" or "must not."
**Example**: (Poor): "You should probably try to not use recursion unless you have to, but it's okay if the solution isn't super fast." (Clear): "Use an iterative solution. Prioritize time efficiency over recursive elegance."
**Why it works**: Ambiguous and contradictory prompts force the model to guess your intent, leading to unpredictable or low-quality output. A clear, direct, and logically consistent prompt provides a stable foundation, enabling the model to expend its capacity on solving the problem rather than deciphering the instructions.
