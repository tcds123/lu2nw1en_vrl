Use Clear, Concise Language and Avoid Jargon

**When to apply**: For all prompts, but especially when describing a novel problem, a specific business rule, or a complex workflow. The goal is immediate comprehension.
**What to do**: Use simple, direct vocabulary. Prefer common programming terms over academic or domain-specific jargon unless it is essential. Break down complex tasks into their fundamental components. Be brief but complete.
**Example**: Instead of "Leverage a heuristic to facilitate the traversal of the nodal network," say "Find the shortest path between two points. You may use the A* search algorithm."
**Why it works**: Abstract or verbose language forces the model to parse unnecessary words and infer meaning, increasing the risk of misunderstanding the core task. Clear, concise instructions reduce cognitive load on the model, allowing it to allocate its capacity to generating accurate code.
